# Writing Room — story, comedy and dialogue

## Short comedy engine
Define **want + obstacle + status clash + escalation + inversion**.
- Want: electrical engineer needs a cable route.
- Obstacle: structural beam and incompatible drawings.
- Status: two competent professionals with overinflated pride.
- Escalation: each insists their design is sacred.
- Inversion: boss demands everything free and tomorrow; both engineers unite against a new problem.

## Dialogue
- Characters speak in distinctive rhythms. Civil: clipped, hot-headed, concrete metaphors. Electrical: teasing, dry deadpan. Boss: confidently impossible demands.
- Include playable actions and reactions: [points at beam], [long stare], [drill stops], [quiet pause]. These beat cues go in direction fields, not spoken text.
- Prefer specificity: "ตู้ไฟอยู่กลางเสา" over "แบบมันผิด".
- If user requests harsh humor, use consensual adult peer banter without slurs against protected groups. Build humor out of professions, situations and absurdity; avoid doxxing real people or false accusations about named businesses.
- Dialogue should fit recorded performance. Estimate speech rhythm only for planning, then time actual audio.
- Use one dominant comedic device per episode: misunderstanding, escalation, rule of three, callback, reversal, deadpan, or superiority collapse.
- Keep an ongoing character relationship and recurring catchphrase, but never repeat the same joke as a substitute for a new story.

## Story beats example (construction comedy)
1. In medias res: electrician raises drill at beam.
2. Civil engineer stops him with exaggerated panic.
3. Electrician reveals conflicting drawing.
4. Both discover owner changed plan.
5. Owner enters asking for zero-cost change.
6. Silent look into camera, final comic caption.

## Outputs per episode
- Hook A/B/C with scores.
- Logline (one sentence).
- Character intent and stakes.
- Spoken script with performance directions separate from voice lines.
- Timing and visual shot list.
- Alternative clean/strong punchline when platform or brand preferences differ.
- A clear callback or satisfying loop only if it helps.

## Serious storytelling variant
For explainers, use **pain → promise → demo/proof → consequence → clear takeaway**, and fact-check any technical, health, financial or legal claim. Do not dramatize unsafe construction practices as instructional advice.
