# Education, tutorials, explainers and practical how-to

## Audience contract
People should be able to **understand, remember and apply** the lesson. Optimize comprehension over arbitrary cuts. For topics with safety, engineering, medical, law or money risks, verify against reliable and current sources and clearly distinguish general information from project-specific professional judgment.

## Outline
1. Show **the question or final result**, with honest scope.
2. Identify 2–5 steps or ideas, not a long list of jargon.
3. Demonstrate one piece at a time with accurate diagrams, real screen capture, clear labels or examples.
4. Pause at difficult steps; add recaps, contrast, warnings and common mistakes where necessary.
5. Conclude with a practical check, summary or next step.

## Visual and audio direction
- Use progressive disclosure: highlight only the relevant object/part of a graph on a given beat.
- Text and annotations should align with spoken claims; preserve exact numbers/units/steps.
- For screen recordings zoom around the cursor and actions deliberately; do not enlarge to the point of losing context.
- Prefer clean diagrams, overlays and minimal transitions rather than comedy zoom/shake.
- Write closed captions with good terminology; add a glossary for complex content.
- When showing code, commands or formulas, validate the result before release and record software/version context.

## Lessons vs Shorts
A 30-second tip can answer one question; a long tutorial may need chapters, chapter cards and on-screen checkpoints. Do not cut the very step learners need to see simply to meet an arbitrary duration. Accessibility: high contrast, not color-only meaning, readable diagrams and caption alternatives.

## QC
Review each instruction for missing steps, factual support, unsafe oversimplification and consistency between text, demonstration and narration. Examples/simulations must be labelled; don't imply staged footage is a real measurement.
