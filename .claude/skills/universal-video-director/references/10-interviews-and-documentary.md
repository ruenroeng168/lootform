# Interviews, podcasts, documentary and source-led stories

## The ethical edit
Preserve speaker intent, chronology and important context. Do not splice words to create a quote someone did not mean. Prefer original audio/video with auditable timecodes. Never invent interviews, firsthand testimony, consent or current event footage. Synthetic dramatization must be clearly distinguishable from authentic footage.

## Edit workflow
1. Transcribe source with timecodes and speaker labels; verify names, proper nouns, dates and significant quotes.
2. Make a **paper edit**: selects of source lines with start/end timecodes; find strong but contextual opening.
3. Structure acts/chapters: question/stakes → experience/evidence → complication → insight/resolution; preserve uncertainty.
4. Use J/L cuts, b-roll, cutaways and room tone carefully to smooth but not distort meaning.
5. Cut filler and redundancy while preserving important qualifiers; do not remove hedges from substantive statements.
6. Add captions, speaker identifiers, source dates, maps/charts/attribution where justified.
7. Check audio consistency, de-reverb/noise only within reason, true room tone, absence of clipped syllables.

## Podcast clips
- A great clip contains an internally comprehensible insight; keep the setup that makes the answer fair.
- If source is audio-only, use designed static/kinetic text, podcast artwork, waveform or licensed b-roll; do not represent synthetic talking heads as the speaker without permission.
- Reframe multi-person footage with shot-aware crops, not blind face-tracking that hides reactions.

## Documentary / investigative
Require dates, source attribution, verification notes, rights to footage/photos, factual claims ledger and response to contested claims as appropriate. Do not use fabricated footage as evidence. Separate opinion, observed fact and allegation. Strong titles can be intriguing without misrepresenting the story.

## QC
Compare source transcript or footage against final quotes; retain a change log and source timeline for reversibility. Watch entire edit for meaning drift.
